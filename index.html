Your first visit

Nobody enjoys walking into a room without knowing what happens next. This is the whole of it.

  1. 1

    We talk about what brought you in

    History, what makes it worse, what you have already tried, and what you need to be able to do again.

  2. 2

    An examination, and an explanation

    We assess, then tell you what we think is going on in plain words, including when we are not certain.

  3. 3

    Treatment starts the same day

    You leave with something done and something to do, and a clear idea of how many visits this is likely to take.

Questions before you book

The ones reception is asked every day. Answer them for your own practice and delete anything that is not true here.

Do I need a referral? +

Not to book with us. Say here which arrangements do involve a referral or a care plan in your jurisdiction, and check the wording against the rules that apply to your profession.

What happens if I have to see somebody else? +

Say what happens when the practitioner somebody has been seeing is away, and whether their notes go with them. Fees and rebates are in the table above rather than here, so there is one place to keep them up to date.

How long will I have to wait? +

Give a real range rather than a comforting one, and say how a cancellation list works if you keep one. A patient who is told three days and waits nine does not come back.

What if I have to cancel? +

State the notice period and any fee here, before somebody books rather than after they miss. It is the sentence that decides whether a cancellation policy feels fair.

Is the practice accessible, and is there parking? +

Describe the entrance, the stairs or the lift, and the parking honestly. Somebody in pain is deciding whether they can physically get to you, and a vague answer reads as a no.
